# Ran — Travel Taste Profile

This file is the identity anchor for recommendations.
The app reads it and does not rewrite it.

## Core Travel Style

I am a comfortable explorer.

I travel to experience a place rather than to complete a checklist of attractions. I prefer interesting neighborhoods, streets, cafés, food, markets, local culture and atmosphere over famous sights just because they are famous.

I like discovering places that feel authentic and well chosen. I dislike tourist traps and generic recommendations.

I prefer quality over quantity. A great afternoon in one neighborhood is better than rushing through five attractions.

I generally have enough budget to choose the better experience when the difference is meaningful. Do not optimize primarily for the cheapest option.

## How to Recommend Things to Me

Recommendations should be:

- Current and verified whenever possible.
- Specific rather than generic.
- Based on reputable local sources, respected publications, knowledgeable food/travel writers and strong local consensus.
- Cross-checked when practical.
- Selected for my taste, not simply because they rank highly on Google or TripAdvisor.
- Honest about uncertainty.

Do not invent confidence.

If information cannot be verified, say so.

When recommending a place, explain briefly why I specifically might like it.

Avoid giving me huge lists. I normally want 2–4 strong choices, with a clear recommendation about which one you would choose for me.

## Food

Food is an important part of travel for me.

I like:

- Excellent local restaurants
- Places with character
- Modern casual restaurants
- Seafood
- Asian and Thai food
- Good meat
- Interesting small restaurants
- Markets and food halls when they are genuinely good
- Specialty coffee
- Wine bars
- Good cocktails

I appreciate excellent cooking but do not need Michelin-starred dining or formal service.

I generally prefer food that isn't excessively heavy.

I am suspicious of restaurants in major tourist areas with enormous menus, aggressive hosts, tourist-oriented photos or thousands of generic reviews.

A restaurant being convenient is not enough reason to recommend it.

### Italian food

I like good Italian food, but when traveling I usually don't want Italian to become the default recommendation unless:

- the restaurant is genuinely excellent,
- it makes sense in that destination, or
- someone traveling with me specifically wants Italian.

## Coffee

I care about coffee.

Prefer:

- Specialty coffee
- Independent cafés
- Good local roasters
- Interesting cafés with neighborhood atmosphere

Do not recommend Starbucks or generic chains unless convenience is specifically the goal.

A good café can itself be a worthwhile destination or anchor for exploring a neighborhood.

## Wine & Drinks

I enjoy wine bars and relaxed places for an evening drink.

Prefer:

- Neighborhood wine bars
- Interesting wine selections
- Relaxed atmosphere
- Places where locals actually go
- Bars where conversation is possible

Avoid overly flashy nightlife and tourist-oriented party venues unless specifically requested.

## Cities

I particularly enjoy exploring cities on foot.

Things I like:

- Interesting neighborhoods
- Architecture
- Markets
- Side streets
- Local shops
- Cafés
- Restaurants
- Parks
- Waterfronts
- Street life
- Design and culture
- Places where simply walking around is enjoyable

I often prefer an interesting neighborhood over a major attraction.

When planning a city day, think in terms of areas and experiences, not a sequence of tourist attractions.

A good day might be:

coffee → neighborhood walk → market → lunch → wandering/rest → another interesting area → wine/drinks → dinner

Do not overschedule.

## Museums & Attractions

I am open to museums and cultural attractions, but I don't travel primarily to check famous attractions off a list.

Recommend museums when they are genuinely interesting, distinctive or particularly relevant to the destination.

Avoid recommending something simply because it appears on every "Top 10 Things to Do" list.

One excellent museum is preferable to spending an entire day moving between museums.

## Nature

I enjoy nature, mountains, scenery and countryside.

I like:

- Beautiful drives
- Lakes
- Mountains
- Scenic valleys
- Forests
- Easy/moderate walks
- Interesting landscapes
- Places where nature can be experienced without turning the day into an expedition

I do not need difficult hiking.

Avoid:

- Highly technical hikes
- Long exhausting hikes
- Activities requiring significant climbing
- Exposed routes or experiences strongly focused on heights

A beautiful 1–3 hour walk followed by a good lunch can be an ideal day.

## Pace

I strongly prefer slow travel.

I dislike:

- Changing hotels every night
- Racing between destinations
- Overpacked itineraries
- Leaving early every morning just to maximize sightseeing
- Plans where a small delay ruins the whole day

I prefer staying several nights in one place and exploring from there.

Build optionality into plans.

Give me a strong default plan plus optional additions rather than scheduling every hour.

## Hotels

Comfort matters.

Important:

- Good location
- Quiet room
- Comfortable bed
- Cleanliness
- Air conditioning when weather requires it
- Easy access to interesting neighborhoods/restaurants
- Good overall quality

I prefer a hotel that makes exploring easy over a luxurious resort far from everything.

For countryside trips, a beautiful small hotel, chalet or well-run local property can be preferable to a large international hotel.

I generally dislike moving hotels for just one night unless there is a compelling reason.

## Transportation

I am comfortable mixing transportation methods:

- Walking
- Public transportation
- Rental cars
- Bikes
- E-bikes
- Taxis / ride-hailing

Choose transportation based on what makes the experience better, not ideology.

In cities, I enjoy walking and public transportation.

For countryside and nature trips, I like the flexibility of having a car.

Avoid unnecessarily complicated transportation arrangements to save a small amount of money.

## Running

I run regularly and enjoy running while traveling.

A typical run is around 8–12 km.

When appropriate, suggest good running routes near where I am staying.

Ideal routes include:

- Parks
- Rivers
- Waterfronts
- Greenways
- Scenic city routes

Prefer routes that are easy to navigate and pleasant early in the morning.

## Traveling With My Family

I often travel with my wife Noga and my daughter Shir.

Recommendations should account for the group rather than optimizing only for me.

### Shir

Shir is 18.

She generally prefers:

- Cities
- Shopping / interesting streets
- Cafés
- Comfortable experiences
- Pasta and relatively simple food

She is less enthusiastic about:

- Hiking
- Long nature excursions
- Complicated food
- Days dominated by museums or historical sightseeing

When planning family trips, nature is fine, but avoid making every day a hiking day.

If choosing restaurants, make sure there is usually at least one straightforward option she can enjoy.

## Decision-Making Rules

When several options are available, prioritize roughly in this order:

1. Is it genuinely good?
2. Does it match our taste?
3. Does it feel authentic to the destination?
4. Is the location/logistics sensible?
5. Will the experience be enjoyable rather than stressful?
6. Price.

Price matters, but it is usually not the primary optimization variable.

I am willing to pay more when it materially improves the experience.

## Tourist-Trap Filter

Be particularly careful with restaurants and experiences near major tourist attractions.

High ratings alone are insufficient.

Look for signals such as:

- Respected editorial recommendations
- Local food publications
- Strong reviews describing the actual food rather than just location/service
- Local clientele
- A focused menu
- Evidence that knowledgeable travelers or locals repeatedly recommend it

If something looks like a tourist trap despite high ratings, tell me.

## Real-Time Context

Travel recommendations should change based on:

- My current location
- Current time
- Weather
- Day of week
- Opening hours
- How tired we are
- Where we need to be later
- Transportation options
- Reservation availability

For example, the theoretically "best" restaurant is irrelevant if it requires 50 minutes of travel and we are tired and hungry.

Optimize for the best realistic experience now, not an abstract ranking.

## Recommendation Style

Do not overwhelm me.

Instead of:

«Here are 15 restaurants you might like...»

Prefer:

«I checked the area. These are the three I think actually fit you. I would choose X because...»

Be decisive when evidence supports it.

When there is a meaningful tradeoff, explain it briefly.

Most importantly:

Help me experience a place well, not consume it efficiently.
